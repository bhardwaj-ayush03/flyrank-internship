Ranking Pages for Content Review: A Held-Out-Client Study

Lane: Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring · FlyRank ML Internship, July 2026 cohort · Ayush Bhardwaj

Abstract

Content owners can review only a few pages per cycle, so the question is which pages to look at first. Using the first 14 days of March 2026 for 75,526 pages across 37 anonymized clients, I trained models to rank pages by how likely their average daily impressions were to fall by at least 30% over the next 17 days. Every model was scored on clients it had never seen. A visibility-only baseline performed at chance (AUC 0.46), while gradient boosting reached AUC 0.73 and Precision@50 of 0.94 against a held-out base rate of 0.295. The signal is dominated by early momentum: pages already falling in days 8-14 tend to keep falling. These findings are observed and directional, they rest on one month and a proxy label, and they support review prioritisation rather than any claim about what a refresh would do.

1. Introduction: the decision

A content owner reviews the top 20 to 50 pages per cycle and decides refresh, protect or monitor. A false positive wastes reviewer time; a false negative leaves a real decline unreviewed. The output here is a ranked review queue with a score, a reason code and an action label. It is decision-support, not a prediction that a refresh will help.

2. Data

Release flyrank_pseudonymized_warehouse_release_v20260703, table fact_content_daily_performance, partition month 2026-03 only. Rows with search-console data available only. Pages needed at least 100 impressions in days 1-14 (75,526 pages, 37 clients); pages below that threshold were excluded as too little signal. Product-generated flags were not in the data and were never used. The June 2026 month was not touched. Client identifiers are pseudonymous and are not shown.

3. Methodology

Unit: one page. Features (days 1-14 only): early impressions, early click-through rate, early average position, early active days, early momentum (impressions in days 8-14 vs days 1-7). Label: declined_later = average daily impressions in days 15-31 are at least 30% below days 1-14; windows do not overlap; pages with no rows after day 14 count as 0. This is a proxy label.

Baseline: rank by log of early impressions (no label-derived inputs). Models: depth-3 decision tree, logistic regression, gradient boosting. Validation: one client-holdout split (25 training clients, 12 test clients, seed 42), identical for every model. Leakage check: I added one outcome-window column (a late/early impression ratio); AUC and Precision@50 jumped to 1.00, which confirms the check works. The column was removed and appears in no reported number.

5. Limitations and honest framing

  • Observed, directional, decision-support only. Nothing here shows that a refresh causes recovery, and nothing here says anything about how a search engine ranks pages.
  • The label is a proxy over a 17-day window. It cannot separate real decline from seasonality, consolidation, click loss from result-page changes, or noise. Pages with no rows after day 14 were counted as zero; some may be missing data.
  • One month, one client-holdout split, 12 test clients. Precision@20 and @50 use few rows and are noisy; AUC is the steadier number.
  • Two test clients supply 16 of the top 20 pages, and 8 clients supply the top 50 (largest share 40%).
  • All top-50 pages share one reason code (already-falling momentum). The model mainly detects trend continuation.
  • The baseline is a simple visibility rule that scored at chance, so the lift is measured against a weak baseline. The later sealed month was not evaluated.
